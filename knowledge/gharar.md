# Gharar (Excessive Uncertainty)
Gharar refers to uncertainty, hazard, or risk in a contract where the consequences are unknown or the subject matter is not clearly defined.

## Core Principle
A contract should not be based on chance or excessive ambiguity. Both parties must know exactly what they are exchanging.

## Common Indicators
- Selling something not yet in possession.
- Contracts where the payout depends on an uncertain event (e.g., conventional insurance).
- Vague descriptions of the asset or price.

## Scholarly Views
- Mainstream view: Excessive gharar invalidates a contract. Small, necessary gharar (like in a house purchase) is often tolerated.
- Conventional Insurance: Most scholars view it as containing gharar because the customer pays premiums but may never receive a payout. Takaful (mutual insurance) is the halal alternative.

Sources: AAOIFI, IFSB.
